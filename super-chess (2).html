♛ Super Chess 16×16

Move history (White | Black)

1. Basics

  • Board: 16×16. Each side has 4 rows: two rows of pieces (32 pieces) and two rows of pawns (32 pawns). White is at the bottom and moves first.
  • Modes (press the mode button before the first move; each press switches to the next one): vs Computer (you are White, the computer is Black), 2 Players on one device, and Computer vs Computer, where you watch two computers play each other. In Computer vs Computer, press ▶ Start to begin, and use ⏸ Pause / ▶ Resume any time. If neither side wins after 300 moves it is declared a draw.
  • Computer level: choose Easy (mostly random moves), Normal, Hard or Expert (searches deepest, up to about 9 seconds per move, and hunts the King). The level applies to every computer player. Normal and above also judge attack and defence: they go after undefended pieces, move or protect their own pieces that are under attack, build pressure on your King to set up checkmate, and (Hard and Expert) search extra moves whenever a King is in danger so they spot mating attacks and escape them.
  • Locked options: once the first move is played, the level and the mode switch are disabled. Press New game and confirm the warning to reset and choose them again. While the computer thinks you can still scroll the board.
  • Your turn: click one of your pieces, then a highlighted square. Green dot = move, red square = capture. Only legal moves are shown.
  • Mirrored army: except the King, Queen and Pawns, every piece type appears exactly twice per side: one on the left half and one on the right half, always on opposite colours of squares (so there is one light-square and one dark-square copy, which matters for the Camel and Bishop).
  • Back row, left to right: Rook, Knight, Bishop, Chancellor, Unicorn, Guardian, Archbishop, Queen, King, Archbishop, Guardian, Unicorn, Chancellor, Bishop, Knight, Rook.
  • Second row, left to right: Amazon, Camel, Serpent, Vampire, Centaur, Fortress, Oracle, Rhino, Rhino, Oracle, Fortress, Centaur, Vampire, Serpent, Camel, Amazon.

2. Check, checkmate and stalemate

  • A King is in check when an enemy piece could capture it next move. The King's square turns red and the status bar shows CHECK!
  • You must resolve a check on your very move: move the King to a safe square, capture the attacker, or block the line of a sliding attacker. Leaps cannot be blocked. You can never make a move that leaves your own King in check.
  • Checkmate: you are in check and have no legal move, so you lose. Stalemate: you are not in check but have no legal move, so the game is a draw.
  • No castling and no en passant.

3. Other global rules

  • Promotion: a Pawn that reaches the far rank becomes an Amazon automatically.
  • Guardian shield: any piece (except the King) in the 8 squares around a friendly 🛡️ Guardian cannot be captured. The Guardian itself is not shielded by other Guardians, so attack it directly first.
  • Blocking: sliding pieces are stopped by any piece. Knight-type and Camel leaps ignore everything in between.

4. Standard pieces

♚King: 1 square in any direction, never into check.
♛Queen: slides any distance in 8 directions.
♜Rook: slides any distance orthogonally.
♝Bishop: slides any distance diagonally.
♞Knight: L-leap (1,2), ignores blockers.
♟Pawn: 1 square forward, or 1 or 2 on its first move (path must be clear). Captures diagonally forward only. Promotes to an Amazon.

5. Special pieces

👑Amazon (Knight + Rook + Bishop = Knight + Queen): all Queen moves plus Knight leaps. The strongest piece; each side has two.
🏰Chancellor (Knight + Rook): Rook slides plus Knight leaps.
⛪Archbishop (Knight + Bishop): Bishop slides plus Knight leaps.
🦄Unicorn (Knight + Camel): Knight leaps (1,2) plus Camel leaps (1,3).
🐪Camel: leaps (1,3) in 8 directions, ignores blockers. It always stays on the same colour of square.
🐍Serpent: moves up to 4 steps through empty squares, alternating one straight step and one diagonal step (it may start with either kind). It can stop on any empty square along the way, or capture an enemy at the end of any step. It cannot pass through pieces. Each side has two.
🧛Vampire: slides like a Queen (any distance, 8 directions). Instead of moving, it can convert an enemy Pawn within 2 squares (only Pawns, never other pieces): the Pawn stays where it is but joins your side and now advances in your direction. A conversion uses your whole turn, and a Pawn shielded by an enemy Guardian can't be converted. Each side has two.
🛡️Guardian: steps 1 square in any direction (move or capture) and shields adjacent friendly pieces (not the King).
🐴Centaur (Knight + King): jumps like a Knight, or steps 1 square in any direction.
🏯Fortress (Rook + 1 diagonal step): slides like a Rook, or steps 1 square diagonally.
🔮Oracle (Bishop + 1 straight step): slides like a Bishop, or steps 1 square up, down, left or right.
🦏Rhino (Rook + Camel): slides like a Rook, or leaps like a Camel (1 square one way and 3 the other, jumping over pieces).

6. Tips

  • Pawns in the second row can't move until the front pawn in front of them has moved.
  • Keep your Guardian near your important pieces, and protect the Guardian too.
  • Zoom with + / − and drag to scroll the board.
